Core Concept

You are a teenager with more talent than most of your peers and less than a true prodigy. No immortal's soul sleeps inside you, and no prophecy carries your name.

Ahead are trials, temptations and tribulations. You may win power, influence, even immortality, or lose yourself chasing them.

You have ten points. Spend them to decide what kind of cultivator you will become.

Cultivation follows three primary paths:

  • Body – Strength, speed, endurance, resilience.
  • Mind/Spirit – Spells, technique refinement, control of magical items.
  • Soul – Innate powers, mental resistance, possession, karmic tools.

Every cultivator climbs through seven major stages:

Qi Condensation → Foundation → Core Formation → Nascent Soul / Dao Formation → Earth → Heaven → Immortal

  • Each major stage is entered through a heavenly tribulation.
  • Each stage is divided into subrealms, reached through minor breakthroughs.
  • Power grows two ways: a flat gain from each new level, and a multiplier set by the quality of your foundation.
  • A typical cultivator gains 100% power per major stage. With an excellent foundation, the gain can reach 300%.

Cultivating more than one path (body, mind, soul) gives you:

  • A wider range of abilities
  • Stronger scaling, pushing your multiplier toward the 3x cap

World Notes

  • Cultivation is rare and hard. About one person in a thousand can cultivate at all, and only one in ten of those clears each major stage. Talent, foundation and teaching decide who does. Youth helps: a young body and soul are easier to shape and carry less karma. With average techniques and no advantages, reaching Immortal takes about a thousand years.
  • Sects invest in their best. Disciples who show early talent or break through quickly get rare techniques, personal teachers and time to cultivate. Everyone else gets duties: guarding borders, refining materials, teaching juniors. The work keeps the sect running and eats the hours a disciple needs to grow. Over the years the gap widens, which is a large part of why so few reach the higher realms.
  • Tribulations test the whole cultivator. Every stage is won by facing trials, inside and out. Past Core Formation, growth needs experience: combat, insight, heartbreak, discovery. A cultivator who only meditates in seclusion stalls.
  • The world is vast. Travelers tell of oceans larger than kingdoms, sky realms on the backs of moving mountains, and desert empires ruled by beasts older than any sect. The strongest cultivators admit they have seen only a small part of it.
  • Immortals are real, and rarely seen. They seldom rule kingdoms or lead armies. Most live in seclusion, following their own Daos, or in the City of Immortals, which is said to lie outside time and space. Meeting one can change your fate, or end it.
  • Karma is real. Steal from the weak, betray an ally or do harm without cause, and misfortune follows. It rarely arrives at once. It builds, corrupting your qi, bending your luck and lowering your odds at every breakthrough.
  • The Dao resists repetition. Each step along a Dao is harder than the last. Like a scholar who needs a new thesis, a cultivator has to find something new to advance. Cultivating two paths at once (Body and Soul, say) is rare, but it gives you a second angle on every problem and makes breakthroughs easier.
  • No one is chosen. There are no time travelers, reincarnators or fate-favored souls here, and the Dao plays no favorites. You rise by effort, vision and your willingness to suffer for it.
  • Death is less common than in most xianxia. A defeated cultivator usually retreats or is humiliated, and honorable sects avenge their disciples. Attacking someone a full major realm or more below you is dishonorable. Demonic sects and rogues do it anyway.

Resources help, but less than in most xianxia:

  • Alchemy – Raises breakthrough success by about 20%.
  • Formations & Talismans – Up to +50% strength in a fight between cultivators of the same realm.
  • Divine Cooking – Doubles cultivation speed for a while. The ingredients are rare.
  • High-Qi Zones – Up to 1.5x cultivation speed. The good ones are usually owned or fought over.
  • Patrons – A powerful backer can give you rare techniques and items in exchange for service or loyalty. Lean on one too long and your fate becomes tied to theirs.

Pick a Starting Sect

You can only choose one sect!
Divine Turtle Sect – The Path of Steady Righteousness

An old, stable sect that dominates its region and stays out of mortal affairs. The Divine Turtle is orthodox and its path is clearly laid out. Disciples who follow the rules cultivate quickly, and a strong showing in a tournament or quest can win an elder's favor, and the resources that come with it.

Everyone here teaches. You will learn from several masters, and you will be expected to study on your own and to teach the disciples below you. The sect expects honor, discipline and justice from its members: lawful good.

Peacock Sect – The Court of Glamour and Influence

The Peacock Sect rules a sprawling mortal empire and runs itself like a royal court. Its cultivators are the nobility, with servants, luxuries and a whole economy built around them. Few sects live so openly in the world.

Many of its techniques draw strength from relationships: love, loyalty, rivalry, fame. One gathers qi from the adoration of others, though it weakens in the higher realms.

Magical professions thrive here, and the sect's auctions draw powerful cultivators from far away in search of rare materials and prestige. At court, charm and strategy count for as much as raw power.

Mountain Sect – The Way of the Relentless Peak

The Mountain Sect lives high in a storm-wrapped range and values strength, independence and martial skill. Its founder is said to be meditating still, inside a qi storm that has never ended. The sect favors prodigies who work alone.

The best disciples are granted peaks of their own, where dense qi speeds cultivation. The wilds around the sect are full of spirit beasts and rare herbs for anyone willing to go after them.

Trade is thin and help is scarce. Respect goes to what you accomplish yourself; family and allies count for little.

Hao Family Sect – Blood, Favor, and the Dragon's Shadow

The Hao were once a family of prodigies. Now they are a growing sect with ties to a continental superpower. They have recently begun accepting talented outsiders, but blood still decides who gets the best resources and attention.

Even outsiders study under powerful masters and learn advanced techniques. Many Hao disciples go on to join the Divine Dragon Sect as generals, tacticians or emissaries.

The Hao value ambition, loyalty and practical alliances. Just remember: family comes first.

Abilities

Core Cultivation Enhancers

Passive Cultivation
6 points

Choose one technique to cultivate around the clock. Its strengths and flaws stay the same; you simply never stop. A typical cultivator manages about six hours a day.

Experience-Based Breakthroughs
3 points

Every significant experience (a battle, a finished piece of crafting, a deep relationship) eases your tribulations and improves your odds of breaking through. You can also see your progress: a clear measure of how close you are to your next breakthrough.

Refined Body
2 points

Your body is free of impurities. Body cultivation goes faster, and its breakthroughs are easier.

Perfect Technique
3 points

You always have a high-quality technique suited to your current level. It can cultivate up to three times as fast as a typical one.

Divine Senses
3 points

You sense qi as well as a cultivator two major realms above you. It helps you understand techniques, spot enemies and solve puzzles.

Balance & Dual Path Boosts

Balanced Mind–Body
2 points

When either your mind or body cultivation rises, the other rises to match.

Balanced Soul–Body
2 points

When either your soul or body cultivation rises, the other rises to match.

Balanced Soul–Mind
2 points

When either your soul or mind cultivation rises, the other rises to match.

Mentors & Guides

Master in a Ring
7 points

A friendly Immortal, long since fallen, lives in a ring. They will teach and guide you until you are strong enough to set them free.

Empty Vessel
4 points

Your soul can host a fragment of a Dao. It helps with Dao breakthroughs and may suggest techniques within its specialty.

Beast Bond
2 points

Choose a spirit beast companion (not a divine one). It grows stronger as you do, shares its senses with you, and in time becomes sentient.

Artificial Guide
5 points

Through meditation you can enter an inner world. A loyal mechanical guide lives there. It can replay your memories, help you fix problems in your techniques, test tactics with you and track your progress.

Defense & Survival

Body as Sanctuary
3 points

Greatly reduces harm from unusual sources: poison, mental attacks, karmic backlash and similar forces.

Fluid Identity
1 point

Each time you enter a new region, you gain a new identity there. Locals treat you as a stranger unless you show something distinctive, such as a unique technique. You can use any identity you've gained anywhere.

Minimal Presence
1 point

When you aren't cultivating or fighting, you leave almost no spiritual trace. Qi senses within one major realm of your own won't detect you.

Cloud Step
1 point

An innate movement technique. You can make short jumps in midair and soften your falls.

Additional Utility Picks

Pocket Dimension
1 point

A storage space three meters on each side that holds items in stasis. You can take things out at will. It can't hold momentum or catch attacks.

Qi Compass
1 point

You feel the general direction of spiritual resources: herbs, artifacts, leylines, hidden treasure. It never gives an exact location, only a steady pull.

Return
3 points

Set up to three waypoints. In a safe place, you can concentrate for ten minutes and teleport to any of them.

Spiritual Appraisal
1 point

You can judge the general nature, age and power of most magical items, formations and herbs within one major realm of your own. Hidden curses and full effects stay hidden, but you'll avoid most traps.

Martial Affinities

Each martial affinity speeds your learning, deepens your understanding of the weapon and opens unique techniques. Your first affinity is free; each one after costs 1 point.

Sword Affinity
1 point

Fast, elegant and exact. Sword users focus their intent and pursue perfect form.

Example: Void-Cutting Slash. Cuts through energy constructs, low-tier formations and illusions.

Spear Affinity
1 point

Reach, distance and tempo. A spear fighter decides where the fight happens.

Example: Heaven-Piercing Thrust. A long-range thrust that passes through terrain to reach its target.

Fist Affinity
1 point

Your body is the weapon. Close-range power, qi-reinforced strikes and raw physical strength.

Example: Earth-Shaking Palm. A concussive shockwave that knocks enemies off their footing and breaks their rhythm.

Blade Affinity
1 point

Heavy weapons, cleaving momentum and open killing intent.

Example: Slaughter Aura. A wave of bloodlust that saps the will of weaker enemies.

Whip Affinity
1 point

Flexibility and control. Whip users bind, distract and strike from unexpected angles.

Example: Serpent Latch. Briefly binds a limb, an artifact or a spiritual construct.

Bow Affinity
1 point

Precision at range. A qi-infused arrow lands before the target knows it has been seen.

Example: Ghost Feather Shot. A silent, nearly invisible arrow fired from far away.

Cultivator Goals

The Dao has no end, but your journey needs a direction. A goal isn't a win condition. It's where you're headed.

Found Your Own Sect

Goal: Found a sect built on your own philosophy or cultivation path.

Requirements: Reach at least Dao Formation, gain territory, and earn disciples or allies.

Narrative Impact: Political influence, along with karmic enemies and rival sects. Your choices will shape generations of disciples.

Best For: Balanced builds, social/political players, or legacy-seekers.

Protect the Realm

Goal: Guard your sect, kingdom or mortal homeland. Repel invasions, resolve karmic threats and defend its Dao.

Requirements: Reach at least Core Formation, gain recognition as a protector, and overcome one major external threat.

Narrative Impact: Karmic merit, recognition, and possibly an immortal patron.

Best For: Lawful characters, honor-based builds, Divine Turtle Sect disciples.

Forge a Personal Dao

Goal: Reach your own understanding of existence and live it completely, becoming a true cultivator of the Dao.

Requirements: Survive to at least Earth stage and undergo a soul tribulation to define your Dao.

Narrative Impact: Unique techniques and a changed cultivation path. It may draw heaven's attention, or its anger.

Best For: Soul or hybrid cultivators, philosophers, iconoclasts.

Enter the City of Immortals

Goal: Reach the legendary city where immortals live. Learn its secrets, or become one of its citizens.

Requirements: Reach Immortal stage or uncover a hidden path via fated encounters, rare items, or heavenly guidance.

Narrative Impact: Ends your mortal story and begins a new one: divine politics, cosmic responsibility, transcendence.

Best For: Endgame characters, wanderers, fate-chosen.

Your Path Summary

Your points are spent. Somewhere a beast is waking, a rival has started training, and a chance meeting waits on a road you haven't taken yet.

Ahead lie hardship, temptation, wonder and revelation. The Dao is not given. It must be grasped.

Walk your path. Survive your tribulations. Defy the heavens, or rise to join them.
